An epidemiological model of monkeypox: model
prediction and control application
> Author

Abstract
Background Monkeypox (Mpox) is an emerging infectious disease caused by the
Mpox virus (MPX Virus). The outbreak of Mpox epidemic has caused global panic
and is now a public health incident. Various approaches have been proposed in the
recent literature to study and analyze the epidemiological dynamics of this
infection and eﬀective prevention and control measures. Using mathematical
model to understand the transmission dynamics and control strategy is a useful
way to understand the prevention of Mpox.
Methods A new compartment model is established to examine the eﬀectiveness
of vaccine on Mpox based on previous studies. Nonlinear least squares ﬁtting is
used for model’s parameter estimation. The impact of a series of preventive and
control measures on the epidemic control is explored with optimal control theory
in conjunction with the oﬃcial data released by the authorities.
Results Firstly, a stability analysis of the developed model was carried out to show
that, under certain circumstances, its equilibrium is both locally and globally stable.
Secondly, based on the reported cases of Mpox infection in the United States
between 2022 and 2023, the model’s optimal parameter values were obtained. A
sensitivity analysis of the model parameters was then conducted to identify the key
parameters that aﬀect the development of Mpox epidemics in the United States.
Lastly, the comparison of control eﬀects under various control strategies showed
that implementing the all suggested four control measures at the same time was
the most eﬀective way to curb the development of monkeypox epidemic in the
United States.
Conclusions This study has theoretical signiﬁcance for understanding and
controlling Mpox virus transmission.
Keywords Mpox transmission model, Parameter estimation, Sensitivity analysis,
Optimal control strategy, United State
Introduction
In the long history of mankind, infectious diseases have always been
accompanied by human beings. Up to now, there have been countless epidemic
virus attacks on human beings, such as plague, measles, cholera, smallpox,
tuberculosis, black death, AIDS, Middle East respiratory syndrome, Ebola virus,
COVID- 19 and so on. e ﬁght against the pandemic virus is arduous and
lengthy, and will always pose threats and obstacles to human life, health and
social development to varying degrees. At a time when the world has just passed
the threshold of the outbreak of the COVID- 19, another outbreak of the global

Mpox epidemic will undoubtedly deal a heavy blow to the world’s health

Mpox, an infectious disease caused by Mpox virus (MPXV) [1], is a novel
zoonotic disease. Not only can it spread rapidly between people, but it can also
be transmitted to humans through contact with infected animals.
Mpox virus was ﬁrst discovered in 1959, when there were two outbreaks of nonfatal variola disease in a population of cynomorphic monkeys from Singapore
received in Copenhagen, Denmark. Magnus et al. isolated Mpox virus from
infected animals, hence the name Mpox [2, 3]. A new Mpox outbreak began in
May 2022. e ﬁrst case of Mpox was reported in the United Kingdom on 7
May 2022, and an individual with a history of travel to Nigeria was conﬁrmed [4,
5]. On May 15, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) reported
a case of Mpox in Massachusetts [6], and since then, the Mpox virus has spread
around the world at an unexpected rate. Within a few months, several
countries, including the United States, were hit by the Mpox virus, and reported
Mpox cases surged, forcing the World Health Organization (WHO) to declare
the disease a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) on
July 21, 2022 [7], causing widespread global public health concern. Outbreak
data reported by the WHO shows that since the start of the Mpox outbreak, 117
countries have reported 93,921 conﬁrmed cases of infection as of January 2024.
Animal to human transmission, human to human transmission and
environmental transmission are possible and potential transmission routes of
Mpox virus. Transmission of the virus may occur when humans come into
physical contact with wild animals such as rodents and primates infected with
Mpox virus (including being bitten or scratched, or coming into direct contact
with the bodily ﬂuids of infected animals during activities such as hunting,
skilling, trapping, and cooking) [8]. Personto-person transmission is mainly
carried out through close contact with infected persons, including face to face
for a long time (such as talking or breathing close to each other to contact
respiratory droplets of infected persons), skin contact (such as touching or
kissing damaged skin to contact body ﬂuids of infected persons), mouth to
mouth (such as kissing) and other behaviors [8]. Compared to past Mpox
outbreaks, this outbreak presents a unique epidemiological feature in that the
virus is transmitted primarily through sexual contact, and in particular the
unprecedented number of Mpox cases transmitted by sexual contact between
men [9–11]. Mpox virus may also live for some time on the surfaces of objects
such as clothing, towels, bedding, electronics that have been touched by
patients, and environmental transmission can occur when people or animals
interact with these items. Especially if the contact has skin lesions or touches
organs or mucous membranes such as eyes, nose and mouth without washing
hands, it may increase the risk of infection.
According to WHO, Mpox virus is a orthopoxvirus, which belongs to the genus
Orthopoxvirus of poxviridae with smallpox virus [12]. Human infection with
Mpox virus causes symptoms similar to, but milder than, smallpox, and the
severity varies from person to person. e incubation period for Mpox is

generally 7–14 days, but may be as long as 21 days [13]. After the incubation
period, prodrome symptoms begin to appear, usually including fever, chills,
headache, muscle aches, fatigue and gland (lymph node) swelling, etc. Lymph
node enlargement is a signiﬁcant feature of Mpox and the main basis for
distinguishing Mpox infection from infection of other members of the poxvirus
family [7]. e signature rash (which looks like a blister or ulcer) will appear
about 0–5 days later, and the rash will last about 2–4 weeks. In most cases,
Mpox is self-limiting, meaning symptoms resolve on their own within a few
weeks. However, immunocompromised groups, such as infants, children,
pregnant women and people with underlying immune deﬁciencies, as well as
people with advanced HIV, may experience more severe symptoms or
complications and even death. According to available data, the mortality rate for
Mpox is between 0.1% and 10%, with the majority of deaths occurring in young
infected people [14].
ere is currently no licensed vaccine and speciﬁc treatment for Mpox virus
infection, but Mpox infection can be prevented and treated through vaccination
against smallpox and antiviral drugs to prevent smallpox. Smallpox vaccine has
been shown to be cross-protective against Mpox, providing about 85%
protection, but currently only about 25% of people endemic to Mpox are
vaccinated against smallpox [15–17]. Since the"Enhanced Smallpox Eradication
Program"successfully achieved global eradication of smallpox in the 1980 s,
smallpox vaccination has been discontinued, and people’s immunity to the virus
has declined signiﬁcantly. In particular, people under the age of 40 lack
immunity to the Mpox virus and are more susceptible to infection, which may
also be the reason for the signiﬁcant increase in the incidence of Mpox in the
past decade [18]. In November 2022, WHO issued the Interim Guidelines for
Mpox Vaccine and Immunization report, suggesting that the recommended
vaccines mainly include ACAM2000, MVA-BN and LC16[19]. Currently, there
is limited clinical data on the eﬃcacy of these vaccines against Mpox, and it is
not possible to know their exact eﬀectiveness and safety. However, during the
study of clinical eﬃcacy, some people who received the above vaccine were still
diagnosed with Mpox virus, indicating that the eﬀectiveness of the vaccine was
not 100% (in fact, the eﬀectiveness of one dose of MVA-BN vaccine was only
35.8%) [20]. erefore, on the one hand, relevant departments should pay more
attention to improving the eﬀectiveness and safety of the drugs and vaccines
currently used for the prevention and treatment of Mpox virus, and at the same
time, more manpower and material resources should be invested in the research
and development of speciﬁc therapeutic drugs for Mpox virus, so as to provide
more practical and eﬀective guarantee for the prevention and control of Mpox
epidemic.
e infectious disease dynamics model plays an important role in the study of
infectious diseases and is widely used to describe the transmission dynamics of
various diseases [21]. Since the global outbreak of Mpox in 2022, it has aroused
wide attention from all walks of life and further promoted the study of Mpox
dynamic model. In 2011, Bhunu and Mushayabasa [22] ﬁrst tried to analyze the
transmission dynamics of Mpox in human and non-human interactions using
SIR dynamics model, solved the disease-free equilibrium point and endemic

vaccination categories and optimal control interventions as well as a

model focusing on quarantine levels and studied the eﬀects of various control
parameters on Mpox infection, exposure and isolation.
In order to study how to reduce the number of infected people with the smallest
cost, so as to propose the best prevention and treatment strategies, many
experts combine the epidemiological model with the optimal control theory [27,
28]. Samreen et al. [29] used a mature optimization theory to study Mpox
disease dynamics under the inﬂuence of several control strategies for constant
and transient variables, and proposed optimal control interventions accordingly.
Peter et al. [30] established and analyzed an optimal control model with four
control variables to study the dynamics of Mpox, showing that implementing
prevention strategies for transmission from rodents to humans is the most
economical and eﬀective. Yap Hushenkan et al. [31] proposed a new Mpox
transmission model, which introduced control variables to simulate the optimal
prevention and treatment strategies. Numerical simulation showed that the
proposed combined control strategy was necessary to prevent the Mpox
epidemic.
Considering the protective eﬃcacy of the monkeypox vaccine, that is, people
vaccinated with the monkeypox vaccine are not completely immune to the
monkeypox virus and are still at risk of infection after exposure to the
monkeypox virus, we wanted to explore the impact of vaccine protective
eﬃcacy on disease transmission. Based on the traditional SEIR model of
infectious disease dynamics, a vaccine compartment was added, and a sevencompartment diﬀerential equation system was constructed to describe the
interaction between humans and non-humans by introducing a modiﬁed factor
to represent the change in Mpox virus infection rate after vaccination. After a
brief theoretical analysis of the established model, the development trend of
Mpox epidemic in the United States during the period of 2022–2023 was
explored using the data of Mpox infection cases in the United States, and
suggestions were provided for epidemic prevention and control.

Model description
Given that Mpox is a zoonotic disease with the potential to be transmitted both
between human populations and from animal hosts to humans, the model
incorporates two distinct populations: the human population and the nonhuman (rodent) population. e total human population (Nh) is classiﬁed into
ﬁve distinct segments: Susceptible humans (Sh) , Vaccinated humans (Vh) ,
Exposed humans (Eh) , Infected humans (Ih) , and Recovered humans (Rh) .
e non-human population is denoted by Nr . Given the current limited
understanding of the dynamics of Mpox virus transmission among rodents, only
two rodent subpopulations are considered: Susceptible rodents (Sr ) and
Infected rodents (Ir) .
We give the following hypothesis: Mpox virus can be transmitted by human
contact, as well as from rodent to human. Susceptible rodents can be infected by
infectious rodents, but not by infectious humans. Rodents are still contagious
and there is no recovery class. Susceptible humans can be transferred to
vaccinated human populations (Vh) by vaccination, but vaccines reduce the risk
of infection and do not provide 100% protection. Infected humans (Ih) transfer
to the recovered human population (Rh) after recovery and gain immunity to
the virus. All humans and rodents are evenly mixed in the same area. e total
human population (Nh) and rodent population (Nr) are constant.

Local sensitivity analysis
e normalized forward sensitivity index [40] is one of the important tools used
to analyze the factors that have a signiﬁcant impact on the spread and control of
epidemics and can measure the relationship between the relative change of
parameters and the relative change of the eﬀective reproduction number. In this
section, the normalized sensitivity analysis of the parameters in the model (2.1)

Conclusion
In this paper, the Mpox epidemic was taken as the research object, after
analyzing the transmission characteristics and research status of Mpox virus,
the protective eﬃcacy of Mpox vaccine was considered for the ﬁrst time, and a
new Mpox dynamic model was proposed to study the transmission dynamics of
Mpox virus by introducing the virus exposure correction factor of vaccinators.
According to the transmission characteristics of Mpox virus, the model is
composed of two subsystems, human and animal, which are speciﬁcally divided
into seven chambers. e human subsystem includes ﬁve chambers, which are
susceptible (Sh) , vaccinated ( h) , latent ( h) , infected (Ih) , and recovered
(Rh);e non-human subsystem includes susceptible (Sr ) and infected (Ir ) .

Firstly, the model is qualitatively analyzed, which proves the positive and
boundedness of model, and the reasonable validity of the model is guaranteed.
e expression of the eﬀective reproduction number of the model was obtained
by using the next generation matrix method, and the existence and stability of
the disease-free equilibrium and the endemic equilibrium of the model were
proved. e stability of the two equilibrium was veriﬁed by numerical
simulation, which fully demonstrated the accuracy of the proof results.

Finally, based on the optimal control theory, four interventions are proposed,

infected people, increasing the coverage of monkeypox vaccine, improving the
protective eﬃcacy of monkeypox vaccine, and improving the treatment level of
monkeypox infection. A new optimal control problem is constructed in order to
obtain the most eﬀective control strategy at the least cost. is paper puts
forward single, double, three and four prevention measures. e eﬀects of
control strategies on monkeypox outbreak were simulated and analyzed. It is
concluded that the implementation of four control measures, that is, the
implementation of four control measures at the same time, is the most
reasonable and eﬀective way to curb the development of monkeypox epidemic
in the United States. erefore, for eﬀective implementation of monkeypox
control measures, policy makers and public health authorities should provide
the following advice: Conduct public awareness campaigns to educate
individuals about the importance of personal protection, such as wearing masks,
practicing good hygiene and social distancing. Strengthen isolation programmes
for those infected to minimize community transmission. Prioritize equitable
distribution of the Mpox vaccine to ensure access to at-risk populations and
underserved communities. Invest in research and development to improve the
protective eﬃcacy of the Mpox vaccine. Ensure proper storage, handling and
management of vaccines to maintain their eﬀectiveness. Strengthen health care
infrastructure to provide timely and eﬀective treatment for measles infection.
e article has the following limitations: Oversimpliﬁcation of rodent
population: only two types of susceptibility and infection are distinguished,
without considering the complexity of rodent transmission (such as incubation
period and secondary infection), which may lead to underestimation or
overestimation of the risk of rodents as sources of infection. Spatial
heterogeneity is not considered: e model assumes that all humans and
rodents are evenly mixed in the same area, ignoring geographic isolation,
diﬀerences in population behavior, or unequal distribution of rodent habitats.
Social interventions are not included: the impact of dynamic changes in
vaccination coverage, isolation policies or public health interventions on
transmission chains is not taken into account. In addition, the control eﬀorts
considered in this study focused only on human populations, and no speciﬁc
studies on animal populations were conducted in this study. Reasonable control
measures targeting animal host populations can be considered as potential
interventions to prevent monkeypox virus transmission. We will further
improve the depth and breadth of our model and research, which is the
direction of our future eﬀorts.
